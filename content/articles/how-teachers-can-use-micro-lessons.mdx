A micro-lesson is not a short lesson because the teacher ran out of time. It is a complete learning cycle with one clear target.

The best micro-lessons answer one question:

**What should the learner be able to do differently after this?**

## The five-part structure

1. **Target**: name the skill in plain language.
2. **Model**: show one strong example.
3. **Notice**: ask students what makes the example work.
4. **Practice**: give one narrow task.
5. **Feedback**: correct one high-value error.

This structure works for English, exam preparation, programming, and AI literacy because it keeps the learner's attention on one move.

## Example: IELTS speaking

Target: answer opinion questions with a clear claim and reason.

Model:

```text
I think online learning is useful because it gives students more control over time.
For example, a student can review a difficult grammar point before class.
```

Practice task: answer one opinion question using claim plus reason plus example.

Feedback focus: does the answer actually answer the question?

## Why micro-lessons work

Micro-lessons reduce cognitive load. They also make progress visible. A learner may not feel that they "improved English" in 15 minutes, but they can feel that they learned how to organize one speaking answer.

That visible progress matters. It creates momentum.

## Teacher checklist

- Keep one target only.
- Use one model example.
- Make practice short but real.
- Correct the most useful error, not every error.
- End with one next step.

Small lessons are not small ambitions. They are a way to make big ambitions trainable.
