Most study habits fail because they are written like wishes: "study more", "read every day", "improve English". A useful habit is smaller, more visible, and easier to repeat.

Try this format:

```text
After [existing routine], I will [small study action] for [short time].
```

Example:

```text
After morning tea, I will review five vocabulary cards for eight minutes.
```

## Make the habit measurable

Track the smallest behavior that proves the habit happened.

| Habit | Bad measure | Better measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Improve speaking | Feel more confident | Record one 60-second answer |
| Learn vocabulary | Read word list | Recall 10 cards |
| Study Python | Watch tutorial | Solve one exercise |

Measurement should be light. If tracking becomes harder than studying, the system is too heavy.

## Use an error log

An error log is a list of mistakes that deserve another repetition. For language learners, it may include grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, or organization. For programming, it may include syntax, concepts, or debugging habits.

Write errors in this shape:

- What I tried
- What went wrong
- The corrected version
- One practice task

## Add spacing

Review the same item after one day, three days, one week, and two weeks. You do not need a perfect system at the beginning. You need a system you can actually use.

> A habit becomes powerful when it is small enough to repeat and specific enough to improve.

Start with one study habit, one measure, and one weekly review. That is enough to build momentum.
